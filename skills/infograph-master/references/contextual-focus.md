# Contextual focus for narrative relationship diagrams
Use when users need to inspect individual constraints within a dense relationship diagram, not for a simple chart. Separate **narrative stage** (which relationships exist now) from **inspection focus** (which visible relationship the reader is examining). Changing focus must not change the factual relationship set, manufacture quantitative weight, or discard context. A focus action pauses automatic presentation playback. Selection and clear operations are reversible; stage change resets stale inspection. Stable IDs bind desktop node, mobile group/individual selector, connection emphasis and evidence/question. For a qualitative case show a question if evidence is absent, not a fabricated result.

Desktop: make group headers and individual labels actionable; use keyboard focus and clear affordance; keep other text legible while reducing nonselected connector salience. Put the contextual detail next to the narrative, with enough space to avoid overflow in presentation states. Do not dim essential text below contrast requirements. Mobile: category aggregation is explicitly labeled; allow category and individual selection with the same semantic IDs, keep controls near the visual, show the current question near the selected item rather than below all analysis. Do not claim individual cable geometry in the aggregate view. Avoid shrinking all labels to fit. Preserve touch targets and restore focus after replacing a list.

QA: verify stage visible counts unchanged by selection, focus subset and clearing, keyboard and pointer selection, pause on selection, consistent semantic IDs across devices, no new desktop vertical overflow across states, mobile control reachability and selected detail position. Check actual active and disabled text/background states in light/dark and forced colors; transparent or complex backgrounds require manual inspection. Device emulation is not the actual host. User approval remains independent. Do not add this interaction merely because it is available; it must answer a reader task.
